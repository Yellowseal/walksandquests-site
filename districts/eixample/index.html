Eixample

Modernist grid of Gaudí

  • 10 quests
  • 47 stops

The Eixample is Ildefons Cerdà’s 19th-century grid — square blocks with cut corners that let in light and air. The bourgeoisie filled it with Modernista houses: Casa Batlló, La Pedrera, the Sagrada Família, and the work of Gaudí’s rivals Domènech i Montaner and Puig i Cadafalch. The quests go beyond the famous façades: gardens hidden inside the blocks, private passages, Barcelona’s Chinatown and the old-school bars of the grid.

A few stops worth the walk

Casa Milà (La Pedrera)

From the quest: Gaudí's Eixample

The stone quarry with no straight walls.

Gaudí's last civil building (1906–1912) before he gave himself wholly to the Sagrada Família. Its rippling limestone front is self-supporting — no wall bears load, so interior partitions could sit anywhere — with balconies of wrought iron twisted like seaweed and a rooftop crowded with chimneys shaped like helmeted warriors.

Jardins de la Torre de les Aigües

From the quest: Secret Gardens of the Eixample

A Victorian water tower hidden inside a city block.

Slip through a narrow tunnel off Carrer de Roger de Llúria and a courtyard opens around a 24-metre brick water tower of 1867, built to supply the first Eixample homes. This was the very first inner-block garden the city clawed back from developers, in 1985 — a rare survivor of Cerdà's dream of a green heart inside every block.

Pere Falqués Lamppost-Benches

From the quest: Hidden in Plain Sight

The street lamps that are secretly benches.

The sinuous wrought-iron lampposts marching down Passeig de Gràcia are the work of municipal architect Pere Falqués i Urpí (1906), each fused to a curved stone bench clad in blue-and-white trencadís mosaic. People sit on them every day without registering that the lamp and the seat are one designed object.
